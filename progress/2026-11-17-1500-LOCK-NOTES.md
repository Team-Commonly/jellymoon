# LOCK-NOTES: a holder lets go behind the system's lock, of the lock that holds its own mark (notes 1, 2, 3, 4 and 9 of the gate on the lock)

**What changed.** On branch `lock-notes`, on main `4fac595`. Core's lock, its tests, three of the binary's tests
that list a folder, two docs.
- **Note 1.** A lock now holds its maker's mark: one line with the process, the moment and a count. A holder
  that lets go asks for the system's lock on `take-over.lock`, waits up to 3 seconds for it while another caller
  holds it, and behind it removes the lock at the name only when that lock is a plain file that holds its own
  mark. It no longer judges by its clock, so a hold past 30 seconds that was never taken over removes its own lock
  too. `take-over.lock` is therefore made by the first change in a folder, not the first take-over.
- **What a holder does without the system's lock** (not had after 3 seconds, or not to be had at all): it removes
  a lock that holds its own mark and is not left behind, and leaves one that is left behind. Leaving a fresh lock
  was the other choice, and it is worse: where no take-over is possible nothing would ever remove it, and every
  later change would be refused until a person removed a file. Removing as before leaves one case open, and only
  there: a clock set during the change (or a hold that ends at its thirtieth second) while the system's lock could
  not be had for 3 whole seconds. `delivery.md` says so under "Not promised".
- **Note 2.** A lock left behind where the take-over's own lock is not to be had at all is refused in its own
  words, which name the two files and the way out. While another caller holds the system's lock the refusal is
  the usual one. The sentence is one, made in `lock.rs`, so the day and the list say it alike.
- **Note 3.** The test with a plain file of mode 000 at `take-over.lock`; it kills mutation 9 below.
- **Note 4.** `take-over.lock` is opened without following a link. On Unix through `rustix` (`O_NOFOLLOW`), for
  which core now names the `fs` feature of the `rustix` it already depends on; `tempfile` builds it with that
  feature already and `Cargo.lock` is unchanged, so no dependency is new. On Windows through the standard
  library's `custom_flags` with the flag that opens the link itself.
- **Note 9.** One line at the end of `progress/2026-11-17-1240-CORE-NOTES.md`.

**Why no caller removes a lock another holds.**
1. What is at a lock's name changes only by a removal and then a create-new; a create-new never replaces.
2. A removal is done by one of three: a caller that waits, behind the system's lock, after a look made there
   found the lock left behind; a holder, behind the system's lock, after a read made there found its own mark;
   a holder without the system's lock, after a read found its own mark and a look found it not left behind.
3. Only one caller is behind the system's lock at a time, and the first two look and remove without letting go
   of it. So between their look and their removal no other removal of those two kinds happens, and by 1 what they
   remove is what they looked at, unless a removal of the third kind falls between the two (8). (Corrected after
   the gate on PR 192: as first written this sentence left the third kind out.)
4. A mark is written by one holder into the one file it made, and no two locks hold one mark. So a holder behind
   the system's lock removes its own lock or nothing: never a lock another caller made, whatever the clocks say.
5. A caller that waits removes a lock only when it is over 30 seconds from now. A lock that is held and under 30
   seconds old by the disk's clock is never that, so it is removed only by its holder.
6. A holder letting go while a caller that waits is at each of its steps. Before its first look, or before it
   asks for the system's lock: the caller has judged nothing that binds; it gets the system's lock before the
   holder, or after, and its second look then finds nothing or the next holder's fresh lock. Between its second
   look and its removal: the holder waits for the system's lock, and then finds its lock gone or another's mark,
   and removes nothing. After its removal: the same. Where the caller found the lock not left behind it removes
   nothing, and the holder then removes its own.
7. Three callers: the third can make its lock only after a removal, and its lock is fresh and holds its own
   mark, so by 4 and 5 neither of the others removes it.
8. What is left open is the third kind of removal in 2, which is not behind the system's lock, in two orders.
   Between that holder's look and its removal a caller that waits could remove the same lock and a third make its
   own, which the holder then removes. Or between a waiting caller's look and its removal, behind the system's
   lock, that holder removes its own lock and a third makes its own, which the caller that waits then removes.
   Either needs the system's lock held by another for 3 whole seconds, or not to be had by this holder while another
   caller has it, and one lock read as in time and as left behind within those moments: a clock set during the
   change. Where the system's lock is not to be had by anyone, no caller that waits removes anything, and the
   third kind is exact.

**How long a caller of `Lock::take` waits.** For the lock: 3 seconds and one pass more (10 ms), as before; the
take-over inside it asks for the system's lock once and never waits. Letting go: at most 3 seconds for the system's
lock, asked for every millisecond, and on Windows up to 3 more for a removal that finds the file in use, as before.
A made wish asks again up to its own wait, as before. No path waits for ever: nothing asks for a lock in a way that
blocks, and the read of the mark is of a plain file and of at most the mark's length and one byte.

open for writing waits on, and on Windows a link, which the open takes as itself.
