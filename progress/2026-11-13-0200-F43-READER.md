# After F43's third gate: the reader's two findings and the byte-order mark

Corrected by `2026-11-13-0500-F43-READER-GATE.md`: three statements below are wrong, and the test was replaced.

- **Done.** Findings 1 and 2 of the gate on PR 143's third round, and the reviewer's note on a byte-order mark,
  in one commit on `f43-reader`. Gated: it changes the reader (`crates/jellymoon-core/src/session.rs`, 11 lines
  net). Six mutations, one at a time, all killed. Doc: `docs/development/reader.md`.
- **1. A log whose length cannot be asked** is read to its end with no bound, as every log was before the bound
  (`session::length`). Decided against saying "this log could not be read": the day has no place for a word
  about one log, a log that cannot be opened is already passed over without one, and a new error would be a
  new thing a day can fail on for a case nobody has seen. Reading it whole loses nothing; the cost is that such
  a log, if it also grows as fast as it is read, has no end again.
- **No test at the binary can reach that branch**: an open regular file gives its length. It is held by a unit
  test of `length` with a made-up error (mutation T3, "no length is an empty log", red there).
- **The bound itself is held at the binary** (`crates/jellymoon/tests/growing_log.rs`): dropped (T1), one byte
  short (T2) and half the length (T4) are each red. T2 was red only in core's tests before; now the log at rest
  ends in a line with no line break at its last byte, and that line has to be in the day.
- **2. The growing-log test waits on files, not the clock.** The made-up session (`fake_writer.rs`) leaves
  `ready` after five lines, types ten megabytes a second until the test leaves `stop`, then types a last line
  and leaves `done`. The test asks the day while it types, then says stop. With the bound in place nothing is
  compared with a time: the day's turns are the first lines in order, at least five, and fewer than the log
  ends with. The 20 seconds `support::jellymoon` gives any command, and the session's own cap (60 seconds or
  200 MB, then `done` says `capped`), are what turn a reader that follows the log into a failure.
- **What still rests on speed, and cannot be helped from outside the reader.** That failure, with the bound
  dropped, needs the session to type faster than the reader reads: 10 MB a second against about 5 measured in
  a debug build here. A reader twice as fast would catch up and the mutation would pass. Nothing a second
  process can see says when the reader opened the log, so only a hook inside the reader could make that side
  exact; none was added. With the bound in place the test cannot fail on timing short of a command that takes
  20 seconds to start and read what was typed meanwhile.
- **The byte-order mark.** A mark before a log's first line is taken off before the line is decoded (2 lines).
  Only the first line: a mark at the start of a later line leaves it not valid JSON, as before (mutations B1,
  B2).
- **Lines.** 160 added, 74 removed, this file and the budgets' reasons among them. Deleted: the 40,000 lines the session typed before the day, the count of
  lines by the file's length, and the bound of half of what was typed while the command ran.
- **Open.** Not run on Windows or Linux here: CI's.
