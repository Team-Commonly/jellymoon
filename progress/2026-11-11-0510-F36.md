# F36: the tap's rules live in core (the gate review of F35, eleven findings)

- **Moved.** The tap is `jellymoon_core::settle` (`tap.rs`), proved at `jellymoon settle`; "Turn on delivery" is
  `turn_on_delivery`, proved at `jellymoon hook install --installed`. `jellymoon-app tap` is gone; `app/src/taps.rs`
  only carries a tap to core and draws (`OnShow::tap`, no window needed). This replaces the 2130-F35 note's first line.
- **F35's entry is unchanged** and its `test` still runs: `app/tests/f35.rs` is now the app's part. Its first and third
  steps still name `jellymoon-app tap`, which no longer exists; F36's steps say where that proof went.
- **Every word sent is drawn.** A day's clash is a kit `whole` choice: drawn in full (wider, then smaller) or left out
  and counted. A tap sends the statement whose drawn text was tapped, compared with core's words.
- **Order of writes**: lock, ruling, settlement. What stands is read from the waiting rulings first, so a ruling whose
  settlement was lost is not made twice (`delivery.md`, "A tap on a clash").
- **Lesson, held by tests.** A door test must fail when its check is removed: the size case puts the padding after
  the JSON, so a read that only stops at the cap would still be believed. Six mutations were run; all are caught.
- **Not proved.** The window's glue from a click to `OnShow::tap`. Windows modes (none are checked there). Two clashes
  of four 200-wide-character statements are counted, not drawn: only `jellymoon settle` rules on them.
