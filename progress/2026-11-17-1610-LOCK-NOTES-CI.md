# LOCK-NOTES-CI: what CI and the gate found on the lock notes: "in use" is a caller at work, and a test that no longer rests on the wait

**What changed.** On branch `lock-notes`, on `67f47da`. Core's lock and its tests, one doc.

**Windows: the new refusal where the usual one was right.** The test that failed there
(`a_lock_that_is_fresh_when_the_wait_ends_…`) was wrong on Windows, by reading: it made the lock "fresh" by writing
an empty file over an empty file, which on Windows leaves the file's time as it was. So the lock was a minute old
to the end, a folder stood at `take-over.lock`, and the new refusal was the true one. The test now writes words and
sets the time. Nothing in that run came from an error of the open or of the lock call.
- The reading found the fault the coordinator asked after, though: an error from opening `take-over.lock`, or from
  asking for its lock, was "not to be had at all" whatever it was. On Windows "access is denied" (5), a sharing
  violation (32) and a lock violation (33) are what a file gives while another process opens, removes or locks it.
  They now read as another caller at work (`not_had` in `lock.rs`), so the refusal is the usual one and never "remove
  both". Only a folder or a link at the name (the look before the open), or an error that is none of the three,
  gives the new refusal there.
- The cost, written in `delivery.md`: a `take-over.lock` that may truly not be written also says "denied" on
  Windows, so there a lock left behind beside it gets the usual refusal for ever, and letting go waits its 3
  seconds each time.
- A second fault found by reading: a holder read its mark once. On Windows a read that finds the file in use at
  that moment fails, the lock was then "not mine" and stayed at its name, and every caller was refused until it
  counted as left behind, 30 seconds. The read is now tried again as the other steps on a file in use are
  (`busy.rs`). On Unix no read of a plain file fails so.

**macOS: two callers refused in the test of many callers.** Not a lock left at its name: then every caller would
have been refused, and the test's own check of the folder would have failed first. Measured on a Mac, one hold of
the build lock, the lock as merged in `4fac595` against this one:

| | before | now |
|---|---|---|
| one take and let-go, nobody waiting | 73 µs | 124 µs |
| 1200 takes by eight callers, unslowed | 5.38 s | 5.59 s |
| the same, slowed `[1, 0, 1, 0]` | 5.30 s | 5.57 s |
| the same, slowed `[1, 0, 0, 1]` (new with the lock notes) | 7.16 s | 7.42 s |

- Letting go costs 51 µs more: it opens `take-over.lock`, locks it, looks at the lock, opens and reads it, and
  closes both, where before it removed one file. A holder never waits on a caller that waits, except one that is
  taking over a lock left behind, which holds the system's lock for a look and a removal.
- A hand-over between callers costs about 4.5 ms either way, since a caller that waits asks every 10 ms; the
  change adds 4% to it. A person with a hook and the app at work meets no refusal from 51 µs.
- The test was the fault. Eight callers take the lock in turn and nothing makes the hand-over fair, so the last
  in line can wait for most of the round, and its wait is 3 seconds. The round already took over 5 seconds on
  a fast Mac before this change, and passed only while no one caller was passed over for 3 of them. The lock notes
  added a third round that pauses 1 ms inside every hold (7.4 seconds), and a runner three times slower did the
  rest. The rounds are now 480 takes each, not 1200. The wait is not widened. The old lock, put under the 1200
  takes with the pauses, had shown 350 faults, so 480 still show the fault the test is for.
- The gate's blocker 1: a smaller round still rested on the 3 seconds. A caller in the test that is refused as
  busy now asks again, as a made wish does, so no runner's speed fails it. What the refusal used to catch is kept
  another way: a lock wrongly left at its name stays 30 seconds, and a caller here gives up after 15 and fails
  the test (mutation g1). Any other error fails it at once.
